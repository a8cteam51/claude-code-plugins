Pressable to WordPress.com Migration Process

Every step we take to move a Team 51-managed site from Pressable to WordPress.com, from the audit to deleting the Pressable site. It describes what happens and who does it, not how. The full commands are in each site's runbook. Steps marked with an condition run only when the audit finds that case.

  • Plugin: pressable-to-wpcom-migration 0.1.0 (PR #15)
  • As of 2026-10-17
  • One real migration so far: kwamebrathwaitearchive.org

Rules that hold throughoutThe decisions every step assumes. Expand to read.
  • Migration only. The site stays Team 51-managed: Atlantis, SSO, monitoring and Team 51 accounts stay. Handing over to the partner is a separate process.
  • One copy. The Migration Assistant runs once, in the cutover window, with the source frozen. It cannot be repeated against the same target, and it replaces the target's database.
  • The database diff is the go-live gate. Unexplained gaps mean unfreeze the source and stop; the live site has not changed.
  • No SafetyNet on production. The quarantine mu-plugin stands in for it on the target. SafetyNet appears only on the staging copy made at the end.
  • Never disconnect or reconnect Jetpack on either site. Restore User Connection in the debug tool is fine.
  • The old site stays frozen after cutover and is deleted only in decommission.
  • Stats and subscribers move on every site. VideoPress, Jetpack plan gaps and WooPayments are flagged work for a person, not holds.
  • Secrets stay out of chat, notes and reports. Keys move from site to site without being printed.

Audit

Read-only. Produces migrations/<domain>/ with the report (HTML and Markdown), the findings and the site's runbook.

Resolve the Pressable siteAgentSite ID, datacenter, PHP version, attached domains.

From the Pressable API through the team51 CLI and MCP.

Inventory the siteAgentPlugins, themes, mu-plugins, constants, Jetpack, WooCommerce, VideoPress, Team 51 tooling, files, cron.
  • A read-only PHP script run through wp eval.
  • Constants are listed by name only. The Atlantis key is reported as a short fingerprint.
  • Also: database size, REST namespaces, files outside wp-content, Pressable host files.
Find related sitesAgentDevelopment and staging clones, and other sites sharing the zone.

Clones are not migrated; they are deleted at decommission. Sites sharing the zone need their records carried over.

Audit DNS and registrarAgentRegistrar, name servers, mail provider, common records. Decides the DNS branch.
  • Uses dig and whois. The record list is a floor: DNS cannot list a zone.
  • Classifies the case: registered at WordPress.com or elsewhere, DNS at Pressable or elsewhere, and whether partner access is needed.
Check deploy stateAgentDeployHQ project and servers, last deployed revision, drift between server and repo.

Decides whether WordPress.com deploys can be connected after cutover, need reconciling first, or stay unconnected.

Gather WordPress.com factsAgentSubscribers, stats, Jetpack plan and products, existing WordPress.com sites for the partner.

Blog ID based. These are the numbers checked again after the stats and subscriber moves.

Gather contextAgentProject P2 posts, Slack, Linear: anything already known about this site.

Recorded in the report with its source.

Build the report and runbookAgentFindings by severity and owner, a verdict, and the runbook for this site's branches.
  • Severities: blocker, decision, manual, handled, info. Each finding names who answers it (developer, TAM, another team).
  • Verdict: on hold, ready after decisions, or ready to plan.
  • Decisions recorded later are kept across rebuilds.

Go live

Moving the domain from one WP Cloud host to another. The first three steps run back to back: from the first, the site is down at its domain until the third.

Move the domain to the new site in DARCcut.domain-movePersonregistered at WordPress.comMove, with the checkbox unchecked. This is the DNS change: name servers switch at once.

A domain-only site it came from is deleted, as expected. The partner still owns and pays for the domain.

Remove the domain from Pressablecut.release-pressablePersonDomain and www alias removed from the Pressable site, so WordPress.com will accept it.
  • Pressable then serves the old site at its staging address; it stays frozen.
  • For a third-party DNS host where the partner is not ready yet, the TXT route alone keeps Pressable serving until they are.
Make the domain primarycut.domainBothReplace the atomic-domain TXT values (apex and www) from Blog RC, then set primary.
  • Connect the domain first if it is registered elsewhere.
  • The TXT replacement was needed even after removing the domain from Pressable.
  • www cannot be primary; it redirects.
  • Setting primary rewrites the temporary address in content; a search-replace by blog ID confirms it.
CheckHome and site URL are the domain; no content holds the temporary host.

The WordPress.com zone starts with the apex A records and a TXT only.

Rollback: old values back, domain back on Pressable.
